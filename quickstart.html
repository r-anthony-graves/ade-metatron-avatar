Quick start

One descent, walked through end to end — then the rules that are easy to get wrong.

Everything in the amber boxes is an example, invented for this guide. It runs at a different frontier than yours on purpose, so you can copy the shape without copying the content. The words in your record should never be mine.

Before you start

Open the Journal tab. Two things sit above the panels on every subtab:

  • the Thought for the Day — a passage and one question, written from the Codex's own record. Answer it in the field beneath; once answered, answer again reopens it.
  • the frontier line, e.g. “harbor — the reef beneath is empty.” That is where a descent is sent. You do not pick it.

The walkthrough

Step 1before you sit

Begin, and write the Intent

On the Intent panel press begin. The session records the frontier the map gave. Then fill three fields.

The objective is the one that matters: write it specifically enough to be wrong. “Seeking clarity” cannot fail, so it teaches nothing. A good objective names something you currently believe and could lose.

Example — frontier: Current
objective
I think I am waiting for more information. I suspect I am avoiding the conversation. Find out which.
correspondences used
nothing to hand; lamp off, window open
state before
restless, slightly defensive
Step 2before you sit

Write the Gateway — including the way out

The door, the route, and the exit. Write the exit protocol now, while you are still able to compose it — not on the way back up.

Example
portal
A door at the bottom of a stairwell, already ajar.
narrative
1. Down, counting the steps. 2. Stop at the door. Note whether the light behind it moves. 3. Go through and stand still. Do not follow anything. 4. Ask the one question in the objective, out loud.
exit protocol
Back up, the same count. Hand flat on the wall at the top. Say aloud what room you are in. Write the Vision Log before speaking to anyone.
Step 3the moment you open your eyes

Write the Vision — raw

Four fields, written before you talk to anyone, including Adé. Do not interpret here; that is Step 4's job and it is a day away. If a field is empty, leave it empty — a descent that returned nothing is a real result.

Example
landscape
A corridor, warmer than the stairs. Smell of paper.
encounter
Someone sitting with their back turned. Did not turn round.
message
Nothing spoken. The impression that they had been waiting a long time and had stopped expecting me.
oddities
I counted eleven steps down and nine back up.
Step 4the next day, not the same hour

Write the Analysis — and the Vision seals

The interval is not delay. It is what lets the subconscious finish and the record gain distance.

Note the two fields the original six sections did not have: expected and happened. Without a recorded expectation, surprise is not available as a signal — and surprise is the most reliable evidence that a belief is wrong.

Example
decoding
The back turned is not rejection. They stopped expecting me.
resonance
The step count. It bothered me more than the figure did.
mirroring
I have been treating the delay as neutral. It has not been.
expected
That I would find a reason to keep waiting.
happened
I found someone who had given up waiting for me.
The moment you record this, the Vision seals — permanently. The Vision panel stops offering its fields and offers an amendment instead: dated, kept beside the original, never over it.

The reason: the format's claim is that the raw record was captured before interpretation touched it. If the Vision stayed editable afterwards you would get a record that agrees with its own analysis because it was quietly edited to.
Step 5within 48 hours

Write the Integration — one action, in the world

This section refuses an empty action. An insight that changes no behaviour was an experience, not a lesson. Come back afterwards and fill in what actually happened.

Example
action
Send the message. Two sentences, no preamble.
due at
Thursday evening
done / outcome
Sent Thursday. They replied in an hour. The delay was mine.
mantra
“They stopped expecting me.”
Step 6later, across journeys

Write the Review

Synchronicities, and recurrence. A recurrence is not a pattern until three things are true: three occurrences counted rather than remembered, the boring explanation written down, and that explanation rejected separately with a reason.

Example
recurrence
Third journey where someone is turned away from me. (3 of 3)
null hypothesis
I am looking for it now, so I am finding it.
rejected because
The first was before I had the objective; I wrote it down without noticing it.

A recurrence that fails the test is kept, not deleted — otherwise the same non-pattern arrives fresh next quarter with no memory that it was already looked at.

The rules behind all of that

Read, not chosen. Your position is the deepest stratum with every stratum above it non-empty; the frontier is the first empty one below. There is no way to set either. You may decline a frontier, but the decline is recorded with a reason and the frontier does not move.
Two things cannot be undone. Writing the Analysis seals the Vision, and nothing in this store is ever deleted — sessions, sections and answers are amended or replaced, never removed.

Nothing you write is graded. There is no verdict field anywhere in the record — no score, no validity flag — so the judgement has nowhere to live. Adé may offer a reading; a reading is a hypothesis, not a ruling, and agreement between the two of you is agreement rather than evidence.

One question, always. The Thought for the Day asks exactly one. A second would hand you the easier one to answer, so a completion carrying two is refused and the day records that instead.

Odds and ends

  • Typing on the Journal tab with nothing owed writes your entry for the day — it does not go to the model.
  • /journal from the Chat tab opens the Journal.
  • Two descents may be open at once; a practice is not obliged to finish.
  • The Codex's own record folds in beneath: the quest board under Gateway, reflections under Analysis, patterns, puzzles and Adé's journal under Review.
