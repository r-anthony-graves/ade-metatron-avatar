Quick start

The Journal tab, and the rules that are easy to get wrong.

The morning

The Thought for the Day sits at the top of the Journal tab, above whichever panel is open. It is written from the Codex's own record — a short passage, then one question turned on you.

  • One question, always. A second would hand you the easier one to answer, so a completion with two is refused and the day records that instead.
  • Answer it in the field beneath. Once answered the answer stands in place of the field — click answer again to revise it.
  • A day the brain was unreachable says so rather than showing nothing. An absent thought and an unwritten one are not the same thing.

The frontier

Beneath the thought is a line like “harbor — the reef beneath is empty.” That is the Codex reading its own depth.

Your position is the deepest stratum with every stratum above it non-empty. The frontier is the first empty one below. Both are read, not chosen — there is no way to set either, which is the point.

The frontier is what a descent targets. You may decline it, but declining is recorded with a reason and the frontier does not move.

A descent

Open the Intent panel and press begin. Six sections follow, filled across days rather than in one sitting:

  1. Intent — the objective, the correspondences you actually used, your state before. Write the objective specifically enough to be wrong; “seeking clarity” cannot fail and so teaches nothing.
  2. Gateway — the portal, the path, and the exit protocol. Write the exit before you go in, while you are still able to compose it.
  3. Vision — raw, the moment you open your eyes. Do not interpret here.
  4. Analysis — a day later, not the same hour. The interval is what lets the subconscious finish.
  5. Integration — one small action in the world. This section refuses an empty one: an insight that changes no behaviour was an experience, not a lesson.
  6. Review — synchronicities, and recurrence across journeys.

The one thing you cannot undo

Writing Analysis seals Vision, permanently. After that the Vision panel stops offering fields and offers an amendment instead — dated, kept beside the original, never over it.

The reason: the format's whole claim is that the raw record was captured before interpretation touched it. If the Vision stays editable afterwards you get a record that agrees with its own analysis because it was quietly edited to.

So the order is Vision first, then Analysis — and write the Vision before speaking to anyone, including Adé.

Nothing in this store is deleted, by design. A session, a section and an answer are amended or replaced; none of them are removed.

What is never judged

Nothing you write is graded. There is no verdict field anywhere in the record — not a score, not a validity flag — so the judgement has nowhere to live. Adé may offer a reading; a reading is a hypothesis, not a ruling, and agreement between the two of you is agreement rather than evidence.

A recurrence is not a pattern

In Review, three things must be true before something counts: three occurrences counted rather than remembered, the boring explanation written down, and that explanation rejected separately with a reason. A recurrence that fails is kept, not deleted — otherwise the same non-pattern arrives fresh next quarter.

Odds and ends

  • Typing on the Journal tab with nothing owed writes your entry for the day. It used to become a prompt to the model instead.
  • /journal from the Chat tab opens the Journal.
  • Two descents may be open at once — a practice is not obliged to finish.
  • The Codex's own record folds in beneath the sections: the quest board under Gateway, reflections under Analysis, patterns, puzzles and Adé's journal under Review.
